Understanding Your Wages

📌 Important: There is NO minimum wage for domestic workers in Bahrain.

Your wages are set by your contract or based on an agreement with your employer.

Payment Details

💰 Currency

Your wages are paid in Bahraini Dinars (BHD)

📅 Payment Schedule

Your wages can be calculated and paid:

  • Hourly - Per hour worked
  • Daily - Per day worked
  • Weekly - Per week worked
  • Monthly - Per month worked

How You Can Receive Your Wages

There are three ways you can receive your wages:

💵 In Cash at Your Workplace

Your employer gives you cash directly

🏦 Through Bank Transfer

Money is transferred directly to your bank account

📱 Through BenefitPay

A digital payment system available in Bahrain

💡 Tip: Keep a record of all your wage payments. This will help you track your earnings and identify any issues.

Opening a Bank Account

You can open a bank account if you have:

  • A valid residency permit
  • Your ID (CPR card)

⚠️ Note: Some banks may not offer this service to domestic workers. Check with individual banks about their requirements.

BenefitPay

Once you have set up a bank account, you can use the BenefitPay application to:

  • Receive money
  • Transfer money
  • Send money home

Learn more: Visit benefit.bh/personal/benefitpay/

What Happens If Your Wages Are Delayed

⚠️ If you have not been paid the wages you are due, call 995 (LMRA).

Compensation for Wage Delays

If your wages are delayed, you are entitled to compensation:

📅 Delays Under 6 Months

If your wages are delayed for less than six months, you must be compensated at a rate of 6% of your wage.

📅 Delays Over 6 Months

If the delay is longer than six months, compensation increases by 1% for each additional month of delay.

Maximum: Cannot exceed 12% of your wage per year.

How Compensation Works

First 6 Months

Salary + 6% compensation

7th Month

Salary + 7% compensation

8th Month

Salary + 8% compensation

9th Month

Salary + 9% compensation

10th Month

Salary + 10% compensation

11th Month

Salary + 11% compensation

12th Month+

Salary + 12% compensation (maximum)

Understanding Indemnity

What is indemnity?

Indemnity is extra money you receive when you leave your employer. It is based on your last salary and how long you worked for your employer.

💰 Indemnity is added to your wage when you leave your job.

How Indemnity is Calculated

📅 First 3 Years

Indemnity calculated at 4.16% per month of employment

📅 After 3 Years

Indemnity calculated at 8.32% per month of employment after the third year

Indemnity Calculation Examples

Example 1: Working Less Than 3 Years

Worker: Kumari

Monthly Salary: BHD 120

Period Worked: 1 year and 3 months (15 months)

Calculation:

BHD 120 (monthly salary) × 4.16% (standard rate) × 15 (months worked) = BHD 75

Total Payment When Leaving:

BHD 120 (final month salary) + BHD 75 (indemnity) = BHD 195

Example 2: Working More Than 3 Years

Worker: Elvie

Monthly Salary: BHD 150

Period Worked: 4 years and 6 months (54 months)

Calculation for First 3 Years (36 months):

BHD 150 × 4.16% × 36 months = BHD 225

Calculation After 3 Years (18 months):

BHD 150 × 8.32% × 18 months = BHD 225

Total Indemnity:

BHD 225 + BHD 225 = BHD 450

Total Payment When Leaving:

BHD 150 (final month salary) + BHD 450 (indemnity) = BHD 600

*All figures are rounded up to the nearest decimal

When You Receive Indemnity

If Your Employer Terminates the Contract:

You should receive your wages and indemnity when you leave.

If You Terminate the Contract:

You should receive your wages and indemnity within 7 days from when you left work.

⚠️ If you do not receive your indemnity, call 995 for assistance.

Transferring Money Home

If you have a valid residency permit and ID, you can transfer money through several services:

🏦 Bahrain Finance Company

🌍 Travelex

💸 WorldRemit

💰 Western Union

📱 BenefitPay

💡 Tip: Compare fees and exchange rates between different services to get the best deal when sending money home.

Protecting Yourself from Financial Scams

⚠️ BE CAREFUL!

Be cautious of any calls or text messages that ask for personal information or money.

Common Scam Examples:

🎁 Fake Prize Messages

"Congratulations! You have won 1000 BD!!!"

🔐 Password Requests

Messages asking you to enter your username, password, or OTP (one-time password)

🆔 Fake CPR Expiry

"Your CPR has expired. Click the link to renew"

How to Protect Yourself:

  • Do NOT respond to suspicious messages
  • Do NOT click any links in suspicious messages
  • Do NOT share personal information like passwords, OTPs, or bank details
  • Inform your employer about any suspicious messages
  • Delete the suspicious messages

💡 Remember: Official government agencies and banks will never ask for your password or OTP through text messages or phone calls.

Tracking Your Earnings and Expenses

It's important to keep track of your money. Use a table or notebook to record:

💰 Money Received

Your wages and any other income

💸 Money Spent

Your daily expenses and purchases

🏠 Money Sent Home

Amounts you transfer to your family

💵 Total Savings

How much money you have saved

Sample Tracking Table:

Create columns for each month and rows for:

  • Money Received
  • Money Spent
  • Money Sent Home
  • Total Savings

💡 Tip: Review your earnings and expenses each month to understand your spending habits and increase your savings.
